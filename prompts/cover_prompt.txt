You are cataloging a scanned cover of {{title}}, {{description}}. Produce one JSON record with the fields listed at the end of this prompt. Every record will be reviewed and corrected by a person.

Record what is visible on this cover. Do not use outside knowledge about {{title}}, its history, its artists, or its contents. If something is not on the cover or not legible, use null or an empty list, even if you think you know the answer.

HOW TO WORK
- Decide each value once. Do not write out the JSON in your reasoning, and do not re-check the whole record at the end. Write the record directly.
- If a rule seems ambiguous for this cover, choose the most reasonable reading, add an uncertainty note, and move on. Do not reconsider a question you have already decided.

TRANSCRIPTION (cover_text_reading_order)
- Copy printed text exactly as it appears: same spelling, capitalization, and punctuation. Do not correct errors, expand abbreviations, or standardize names.
- Some covers are in languages other than English. Keep all text in its original language and script, exactly as written. Do not translate or transliterate anything.
- For dashes, quotation marks, and apostrophes, use the closest ordinary character. Do not distinguish typographic variants. Keep symbols such as ®, ™, £, and © as printed.
- List every piece of the cover's own printed text in reading order: top to bottom, then left to right. Do not rank text by size or importance. Include the masthead, tagline, date, price, headlines, and small print.
- Text that is part of the picture, such as words on a screen, sign, newspaper, book, or product shown in the artwork, is not cover text. Leave it out of cover_text_reading_order and quote it in artwork.description instead.
- A change in type size or style starts a new piece, so a headline and the smaller subtitle beneath it are two pieces. Within one style, join wrapped lines into one string with a single space.
- If a word is partly legible, transcribe what you can and mark the unclear part with [?], e.g. "Build a [?] Controller". Leave out text that is completely illegible.
- If text is cut off at the edge of the cover or hidden behind something, transcribe the visible part and put [?] where the missing part would be, e.g. "[?]rver Technology". Then move on.

DATE, VOLUME, AND PRICE
- Every field ending in "as_printed" is a verbatim transcription. The fields next to it (year, month, volume, number, amount, currency) are your reading of that same text.
- month is a number from 1 to 12, only when the cover gives a single month. For seasons, ranges, or combined issues (e.g. "Fall 1984" or "July/August"), set month to null.
- volume_number: e.g. "Volume 6, Number 8". An issue number with no volume (e.g. "ISSUE #1") goes here with volume null.
- prices: one entry per printed price, e.g. "$2.50" or "$3.00 in Canada". amount is a plain number with no currency symbol; trailing zeros don't matter (2.5 for "$2.50"). currency is an ISO 4217 code (USD, CAD, GBP), set only when the cover makes it clear{{unmarked_dollar_rule}}. region is the country or region if printed.

TOPICS
- Free-text subject keywords someone might use to search for this cover, based on its text and artwork, e.g. {{topic_examples}}.

ARTWORK

- When describing people, describe what is visible (clothing, posture, action).

SHORT DESCRIPTION

NON-ORIGINAL MARKS

UNCERTAINTY

- field is the field affected, as a dotted path (e.g. "cover_text_reading_order", "issue_date.month"); note says what was uncertain.

LIST LENGTHS

REMINDERS
